## How the case surfaced

PicsArt, an online photo-editing platform, flagged the account and the images Fairchild had created and reported them to the National Center for Missing and Exploited Children (NCMEC). NCMEC passed the information to the Colorado Internet Crimes Against Children Task Force, and the Boulder County Digital Forensics Lab took over the investigation. A search warrant was served at Fairchild's home on January 15, 2026. He turned himself in on January 30, 2026 and was booked into the Boulder County Jail with bond set at $50,000.

Boulder County described this as one of its forensics lab's first full investigations involving AI.

## The statute

All 23 counts are under Colorado law: 11 counts of sexual exploitation of a child by producing sexually exploitative material under C.R.S. § 18-6-403(3)(b), a class 3 felony; 11 counts of possession under § 18-6-403(3)(b.5), a class 5 felony; and one count under § 18-6-403(5.5)(a), the extraordinary-risk enhancer for material involving a child under 12. No federal charges have been reported.

The offences charged are older than generative image tools. In 2025, though, Colorado added computer-generated depictions of an identifiable child to the statute's definition of sexually exploitative material (SB25-288).

## Altered images

The sheriff's office says photographs of real children were altered using AI. In Ashcroft v. Free Speech Coalition (2002), the Supreme Court held that wholly virtual images that are not obscene are protected speech. Images made by altering photos of identifiable real children fall within the federal definition in 18 U.S.C. § 2256(8)(C), and courts have upheld prosecutions for them. The case is a state prosecution, but federal law draws the same line between altered and wholly generated images.
