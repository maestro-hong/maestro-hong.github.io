## Correction

Most reports say Smith created hundreds of thousands of songs with AI. The indictment says he bought them.

The error began with the government. The US Attorney's plea announcement says Smith generated the songs using AI, which contradicts the indictment the same office filed 18 months earlier. News reports repeated the newer statement, often while linking to the indictment in the same paragraph.

The supplier was the chief executive of an AI music company, whom the indictment names as a co-conspirator. Under a Master Services Agreement dated February 1, 2019, the CEO agreed to supply 1,000 to 10,000 songs a month. Smith paid $2,000 a month or 15% of the streaming revenue.

## Why he needed AI songs

Smith had been running bot accounts since 2017 and had tried selling fraudulent streams to other musicians. Platforms flag tracks with implausible play counts. A billion streams on one song gets noticed; the same billion spread across tens of thousands of songs does not. The more songs he had, the more streams he could hide.

In emails to co-conspirators in late 2018 and 2019, quoted in the indictment, Smith said he needed a large quantity of songs, each carrying a small number of streams, and needed them quickly to work around anti-fraud systems. He also wanted enough catalogue to avoid overrunning individual songs and losing them. In June 2019 he asked his supplier for another 10,000 songs so the streams could be spread wider.

## Operational detail

At the peak, roughly 10,000 bot accounts were running, registered with bulk-purchased email addresses under fabricated names. Family plans cut the cost of keeping so many accounts. VPNs concealed that the accounts all operated from one residence. Payments ran through a debit-card service. Smith told the service he was issuing cards to company employees, and the arrangement was funded with $1.3 million in fraud proceeds.

The audio files arrived with randomized filenames. Smith attached invented song titles and artist names to the files.

## Forfeiture

In a February 2024 email, Smith claimed more than four billion streams and $12 million in royalties since 2019. The plea agreement sets forfeiture at $8,091,843.64. Smith's $12 million, the $8.09 million forfeiture and the $10 million in some coverage measure different things.

## The 2023 denial

In 2023, when the Mechanical Licensing Collective paused payments and challenged him, Smith and his representatives denied manipulating streams and said the songs were human-authored, not computer-generated.

## Enforcement

Smith was charged on September 4, 2024 with wire fraud conspiracy, wire fraud and money laundering conspiracy. Each count carries a maximum of 20 years. On March 19, 2026 Smith pleaded guilty to one count of conspiracy to commit wire fraud under a superseding information charging 18 U.S.C. § 371, which carries a maximum of five years.

On September 22, 2026, the defence filed a sentencing memorandum asking for probation, arguing that the losses were so thinly spread that no artist suffered any perceptible harm. The Sentencing Guidelines range is 46 to 57 months, but the Probation Office recommended 24 months.

**Open** No sentencing date has been set. No public charging document has been found for the AI music company executive.
