## Allegations

Between 2024 and 2026, Jacob Soall, Sharon Hazan, Guy Sheetrit and others allegedly targeted employees at various companies, often in accounting departments. The Associated Press, citing court documents, reports that the approach usually began with an email that appeared to come from the company's chief executive asking for help with a confidential financial transaction.

The victims were then persuaded on a phone or video call in which the CEO's voice, and sometimes image, was mimicked. The "CEO" told the employee to contact lawyers at a large international law firm who were supposedly working on the deal. Those lawyers were impersonated too, and one of them told the employee to wire a large sum to an account the group controlled.

Prosecutors say Soall mainly posed as a British attorney and kept a handwritten script titled "Lawyer Call", which included language designed to convince employees not to discuss the calls with colleagues. Hazan and Sheetrit allegedly laundered the proceeds. The money went to accounts in the United States, Spain, Portugal, Greece, the United Arab Emirates, Singapore, Austria, Slovakia and China before being converted to cryptocurrency.

Soall was arrested in Spain on June 17, 2026, Hazan in Spain on August 5, 2026 and Sheetrit in the United Kingdom on September 7, 2026. Each faces seven counts: conspiracy to commit wire fraud, wire fraud, two counts of aggravated identity theft, money laundering conspiracy and two counts of money laundering. Hazan was convicted of conspiracy to commit bank fraud in the same court in 2008.

## The AI claim

The release describes the method only in general terms: the group allegedly used AI to mimic executives' voices and appearance and to impersonate real lawyers. The AI claim appears in the charges themselves as well as in the press statement. The release calls the technology "super intelligence" throughout.

## The checks it got past

Staff approving a payment often rely on two informal checks: they recognise the caller's voice, and someone else confirms the deal is real. Here the same group supplied both.

The script prosecutors describe also told employees to keep the calls to themselves, which cut out a third check, since a second approver can only object to a payment they hear about.

## The numbers

Six victims are named in the release: hospital systems in Missouri ($4.8 million) and California ($3.0 million), a bank in Rhode Island ($2.7 million), a UK financial technology company ($11.6 million), a Massachusetts mutual fund company ($4.6 million) and a Florida pet food company ($5.2 million). Together they account for about $32 million. Roughly three quarters of the alleged $129 million comes from victims the release does not identify.

## Open
