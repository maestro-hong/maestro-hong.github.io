## Allegations

Between 2024 and 2026, Jacob Soall, Sharon Hazan, Guy Sheetrit and others allegedly targeted employees at various companies, often in accounting departments. The Associated Press, citing court documents, reports that the approach usually began with an email that appeared to come from the company's chief executive asking for help with a confidential financial transaction.

A phone or video call were the methods used to persuade the victims where the CEO's voice and sometimes image was mimicked. The "CEO" then told the employee to contact lawyers at a large international law firm who were supposedly working on the deal. The lawyers were also impersonated and one of them provided the instruction to wire a large sum to an account controlled by the group.

Prosecutors say Soall mainly acted as a British attorney and kept a handwritten script titled "Lawyer Call", which included instructions to stop employees from discussing the calls with colleagues for confidentiality. Hazan and Sheetrit allegedly laundered the proceeds. The money went to accounts in the United States, Spain, Portugal, Greece, the United Arab Emirates, Singapore, Austria, Slovakia and China before being converted to cryptocurrency.

Soall was arrested in Spain on June 17, 2026, Hazan in Spain on August 5, 2026 and Sheetrit in the United Kingdom on September 7, 2026. Each faces the following seven counts: conspiracy to commit wire fraud, wire fraud, two counts of aggravated identity theft, money laundering conspiracy and two counts of money laundering. Hazan was convicted in the same court of when he was charged for conspiracy to commit bank fraud in 2008.

## The AI-enabled crime

The report only describes the method at a surface level, which claims the group used AI to mimic executives' voices and visual appearance and to impersonate real lawyers. That places the claim on a charging document rather than an official statement alone.

The release calls the technology "super intelligence" throughout. The conduct the criminals used is described as voice and image impersonation and no source names the tools used or explains how the executives' voice samples were obtained.

## The checks it got past

Most payment approvals are based on two informal checks: the employee recognises the voice of the person requesting the fund and a second party confirms the deal is authentic. In this case, both were supplied by the same group so the corroboration came from inside the scheme.

The confidentiality instructed by the criminal in the script targets a third check. Dual approval and peer review only work if a colleague hears about the payment.

## The numbers

Six victims are named in the release: hospital systems in Missouri ($4.8 million) and California ($3.0 million), a bank in Rhode Island ($2.7 million), a UK financial technology company ($11.6 million), a Massachusetts mutual fund company ($4.6 million) and a Florida pet food company ($5.2 million). Together they account for about $32 million. Roughly three quarters of the alleged $129 million sits with victims the release does not identify.
