## What happened

The Press Trust of India (PTI) reported on June 17, 2026 that officers from the Sarita Vihar Police Station in Delhi arrested five Delhi residents in Bareilly, Uttar Pradesh, on Tuesday. It says a branch manager at a private bank in Jasola alerted police to suspicious transactions and that the withdrawal of nearly ₹9 lakh (900,000 rupees) was prevented. It also says police registered a first information report (FIR) under provisions of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, India's criminal code, but gives no section numbers.

PTI describes the five men as a commission-based network that allegedly arranged and operated bank accounts used to route and withdraw money from cyber frauds. It says police linked the Delhi case to a fraud reported in Mumbai, where a private company was allegedly defrauded of about ₹10.40 crore (104 million rupees) in 63 transactions between June 3 and June 15. The Mumbai case was registered by the South Cyber Police Station of the Mumbai Crime Branch, according to PTI.

## The AI claim

The Logical Indian says that, according to police, "the accused" cloned the managing director's voice and contacted the company's deputy general manager. Its headline says the gang reportedly used AI voice cloning, and its text says the deputy general manager reportedly authorised transfers without the usual layers of verification. NewsBytes says AI was used to clone the voice and trick the deputy general manager into transferring over ₹10 crore. It names no source.

PTI does not mention AI, a cloned voice, a managing director or a deputy general manager. On the Mumbai case it says the investigation showed "a direct link to a major CEO/social media impersonation fraud reported in Mumbai". None of the three reports we read names a voice-cloning tool or says where the audio came from.

## Open

**The police statement** PTI cites unnamed police and officials and refers to no release. Our searches found no Delhi Police or Mumbai Police release, and we did not check either force's website. The Logical Indian links to a June 17 India Today report that we could not open, so we do not know whether The Logical Indian's police attribution comes from that report. We read only NewsBytes's short summary page.

**The names** All three reports name some or all of the five men. In what we read, none says who released the names. We have left them out because we have seen no agency release or court document that names them.

**The five men** PTI does not link them to the cloned voice or to contacting the deputy general manager. The Logical Indian says "the accused" did both, and its headline says "Delhi Gang". The NewsBytes headline says five Delhi men used the clone. Both outlets present the arrested group as the cloners. Neither says which of the five did what.

**The numbers** PTI says the company was allegedly defrauded of about ₹10.40 crore in 63 transactions. The Logical Indian and NewsBytes say over ₹10 crore went to 63 bank accounts. Transactions and accounts are different counts, and we cannot reconcile them from what we read.

**The checks** The only check with a reported result is the bank branch manager's alert, which PTI says prevented a withdrawal of nearly ₹9 lakh. PTI does not say that money was part of the ₹10.40 crore. None of the three reports we read says what the usual layers of verification were, which check failed, whether any other withdrawal was prevented or whether any money was recovered.

**The detection** NewsBytes says a bank manager's alert led to one man's arrest. The Logical Indian says the fraud came to light when one man was detained at a bank. The Logical Indian also says Mumbai Police registered a case after the company complained. None of the reports we read says when the company complained or how it found the loss.

**The status** The Logical Indian article, our newest source, was published on June 19 and updated on July 29. We ran one search for later coverage and found none. We did not check court records, so we list the case as investigating based on the June reports.

**The date** Our incident date is June 17, 2026, the date of the earliest report we read (PTI, as carried by The Patriot). PTI says the arrests were on Tuesday. June 17, 2026 was a Wednesday, so the arrests were probably on June 16. That is our inference.
