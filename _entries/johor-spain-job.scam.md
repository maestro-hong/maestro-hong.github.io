## The operation

A six-room bungalow in Gelang Patah, the Iskandar Puteri district near
the Singapore border had been running scam operation since February.
33 occupants worked as agents, sourcing targets on Telegram
and offering jobs to victims that did not exist.

The scam methodology was nothing new. Victims were offered employment at several salary
tiers, with the higher salaries requiring the purchase of a more expensive
package. They bought packages. There was no job.

Police raided around the bungalow 11:50PM on May 14 and seized a total of 35 phones, 35 laptops, 10
desktop computers and 12 modems, valued at about RM65,000. All 35 tested
negative for drugs. None had a prior record.

## What the AI actually did

The district police chief said the syndicate used AI and prepared language
templates to translate operator speech from Mandarin to English and
inbound Spanish to English.

The operators spoke Mandarin. The victims were located in Spain. The translation layer works as a bridge
to make the scam operate.

It is worth noting what the police account does not settle. The translation
described runs Mandarin into English and inbound Spanish into English, which
makes English the bridge between the languages. Whether victims were native Spanish or English-speakers living
in Spain is not stated anywhere.

The AI capability was not efficiency. It was market access.
A criminal who previously had been bounded by the languages can now work any language market. Every
assumption about where a given syndicate's victims will be found rests on a
constraint that has quietly stopped applying.

## The part nobody reported

On May 27 2026, 31 of the accused pleaded guilty in the Johor Bahru
Magistrates' Court and were fined RM3,500 each. All fines were paid in full. 
Four others remained under investigation pending identity
verification. The fine was smaller than one month's
wages for average person.

## Why the charge changed, and what it suggests

The 35 were initially remanded under Section 420 of the Penal Code, which
covers that dishonesty induces the delivery of property and carries
up to ten years. The prosecution that followed proceeded under Section 417,
simple cheating, read with Section 120B for criminal conspiracy. It is a
materially lesser offence which in practice draws a fine.

No source explains the change. Offered here as inference rather
than fact, is that the more serious charge requires proving that identified
victims actually parted with property, and every victim in this case was in
Spain. No Spanish complainant appears anywhere in the public record. No loss
figure was ever published. Nothing indicates that Spanish authorities were
involved at any stage or that any Spanish victim was ever notified.

The same translation capability that allows this syndicate reach victims on
another continent also placed those victims beyond the evidentiary reach of
the prosecutors who would have needed them. The technology extended the
offence across a border that the case against it could not cross.

## A comparison worth having

In July 2026, the same court sentenced two Chinese nationals over Forest City love
scams, charged under the identical sections of one month's imprisonment with
a RM7,000 fine each.

This record does not know why. It is noted because the disparity is real and
because nobody appears to have asked about it.
