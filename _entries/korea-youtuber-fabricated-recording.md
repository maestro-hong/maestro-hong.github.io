## What is alleged

Between March and May 2025, the YouTube channel Garosero Research Institute
(가로세로연구소, known as Gaseyeon) broadcast claims that actor Kim Soo-hyun
had dated the late actress Kim Sae-ron for about six years from the age of 15,
and that pressure from his side to repay a debt was behind her death. On May 7
2025, at a press conference held with her family, the channel's head played a
recording presented as her voice.

Kim Soo-hyun has said the two dated only after she became an adult. His side
filed criminal complaints and civil suits, including a claim for 12 billion won
in damages against the channel's head and members of her family.

Seoul's Gangnam Police Station concluded that he had not dated her while she was
a minor. Police also found that 11 KakaoTalk screenshots from 2016, which the
channel had received from the family, had been edited in seven places,
including changing the other person's name to "Kim Soo-hyun". According to the
arrest warrant application as reported, the motive was YouTube revenue.

None of this has been tested at trial.

## The AI claim

The headlines said AI. The record underneath them is less certain.

- **August 2025.** Police asked the National Forensic Service to examine the
  recording.
- **November 2025.** The Service returned a result of "cannot be determined"
  on whether the recording was generated or altered with AI. It had received a
  copy rather than the original, and noise in the audio limited the analysis.
- **May 2026.** Police said the recording appeared to have been generated by
  AI. No report describes how they reached that view beyond the forensic
  result. The Public reports that police relied on the file's distribution
  route, the existence of different versions and witness statements.
- **June 2026.** JTBC reported that the Supreme Prosecutors' Office's
  scientific investigation division found a break in the audio signal and
  different frequency bands for the male and female voices. Its conclusion, as
  reported, was that a woman's voice recorded on another device may have been
  played back and re-recorded. AI is not mentioned in those findings.
- **July 2026.** The Public, which reviewed the indictment, reports that it
  calls the file a "manipulated voice file" and does not use the word "AI".
  Prosecutors cited abnormal editing traces, statements the deceased could not
  plausibly have made, and the conduct of the anonymous sender, who refused
  in-person verification, demanded money and gave the same material to a
  party with opposing interests.

Seoul Shinmun's report of the indictment on June 23 did say he allegedly used
generative AI to manipulate her voice. The later reports of the indictment's
wording, and the defendant's own argument in court on October 15 that the
indictment dropped the AI and knowledge language used in the arrest warrant,
point the other way. This record has not read the indictment.

The prosecution does not need the method. The charges turn on whether he
broadcast material he knew or should have known was false, not on how the file
was made. That is also why the AI question may never be decided in court.

The claim is recorded here at the level the sources support: a police
statement that the recording appeared to be AI-generated, not confirmed by
either forensic examination reported so far.

## The charges

Prosecutors in the Women and Children Crime Investigation Division 2 of the
Seoul Central District Prosecutors' Office indicted him while detained on June
23 2026. Reported charges are defamation under the Information and
Communications Network Act, distributing illegally filmed material under the
Act on Special Cases Concerning the Punishment of Sexual Crimes, and a
violation of the Stalking Punishment Act. The Public also lists attempted
coercion and intimidation. No source describes the material behind the
sexual-crimes count, and this record does not speculate.

In September, the court approved a prosecution request to freeze about 169
million won in assets as proceeds of crime. Prosecutors put his earnings at
about 169.4 million won, including at least 54.4 million won in donations and
at least 115 million won in YouTube advertising revenue.

## Detention and trial

The Seoul Central District Court issued the arrest warrant on May 26 2026,
citing the risk that he would destroy evidence or flee. Police referred the
case to prosecutors on June 4. A later request to review the legality of his
detention was rejected.

The case is before Criminal Panel 26 of the Seoul Central District Court. He
asked for a jury trial; the court confirmed on October 15 that the case will
not be tried by jury. At the second pretrial hearing that day he asked for
bail, citing his mental health. Prosecutors opposed it, arguing that he would
resume broadcasting and harm the victims. No ruling on bail has been reported.
His lawyers have also filed an objection to a forensic examination of his phone
carried out without his participation. The pretrial hearing continues on
October 28 2026.

He denies the charges and has said he is confident of acquittal. The Public
reports that he faces eight other prosecutions.

## Why this entry exists

An entry requires that AI played a material role in committing, attempting or
enabling a crime. Here that role is claimed by the police and repeated in
international coverage, but it is not established by the forensic evidence
reported so far, and the charging document reportedly leaves it out.

The case is logged for that reason. It is the clearest example in this record
of an AI attribution growing from a police statement into a global headline
while the formal case moved away from it. If the trial establishes that the
recording was spliced or re-recorded rather than generated, the entry will be
closed as a case in which AI was claimed but not used.

## A note on naming

Kim Soo-hyun and Kim Sae-ron are named because the actor and his agency have
addressed the allegations publicly under his name, and the case cannot be
described without them. This record could not locate a court document or agency
release naming the defendant. Korean and international media name him widely;
he is identified here by his role as head of the channel.
