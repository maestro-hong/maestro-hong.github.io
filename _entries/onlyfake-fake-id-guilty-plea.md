## What happened

Yurii Nazarenko, a 27-year-old Ukrainian, pleaded guilty in Manhattan federal court on February 26, 2026, to conspiracy to commit fraud involving identification documents. The charge carries up to 15 years. The case is before US District Judge Margaret Garnett.

Prosecutors say he ran OnlyFake, a website that sold digital images of fake IDs from about 2021 to 2024. A customer picked the document and chose whether it should look like a flat scan or a photo taken on a table. The catalogue covered driver's licences for all fifty US states, US passports and passport cards, Social Security cards, and passports from some 56 other countries. Payment was in cryptocurrency, and bulk orders ran to 1,000 images at a time.

Prosecutors put the total at no fewer than 10,000 images and the takings at hundreds of thousands of dollars. Nazarenko agreed to forfeit $1.2 million in proceeds. He had been extradited from Romania in September 2025.

The images were meant, prosecutors said, to get customers past identity checks at banks and crypto exchanges and to hide who was moving the money. The US Attorney's Office for the Southern District of New York described the charges as among the first ever brought over digital fake identification documents.

## The AI claim

The Justice Department's announcement talks about "digital fake IDs" and photos of documents and never uses the word AI. An FBI official quoted in it says only that Nazarenko exploited technology.

BleepingComputer called OnlyFake "AI-powered" but did not say how the images were made. Biometric Update mentioned generative AI only in passing.

404 Media wrote about the site in February 2024 and described its images as made with "neural networks." In November 2026 it published an interview with Nazarenko. In the free excerpt he says he started out editing passports by hand in Photoshop, which could take a week for one good fake, and then built a site that filled in customers' details automatically. Once generative AI tools became widely available, he says, he started using them as well. We have seen only the free excerpt; the full interview is paywalled. dev.ua, summarising it, adds that his team bought scans of real documents to build a sample set.

## The sentence

Sentencing was set for June 26, 2026. dev.ua, again summarising 404 Media, reports that the court imposed time served, about a year in custody, and that immigration officers then held him and deported him. We found no record of the sentence from the Justice Department or on the court docket.

## Open

**Software** We do not know what generated the images, or whether most of the work came from templates. No coverage names a model or a tool.

**Charge** The announcement cites no statute. The indictment is on DocumentCloud, and we have not reviewed it.

**How he was found** BleepingComputer says undercover FBI agents bought documents in May and June 2024, which the Justice Department did not mention. dev.ua, citing Nazarenko, says a payment to an exchange account in his real name gave him away.

**Money and customers** Other outlets reported different totals; we have used the Justice Department's. Prosecutors named no customers. The claim that North Korean IT workers used the site comes only from Nazarenko, relayed by dev.ua.

**Identity checks** Prosecutors have not named a bank or exchange the fakes were used on, or a check they beat.
