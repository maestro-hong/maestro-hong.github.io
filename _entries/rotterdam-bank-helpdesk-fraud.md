## What happened

In September 2026, Dutch banks reported to the Electronic Crimes Task Force that customers were losing money to a helpdesk scam the banks never seen before. The task force is formed with a standing group of banks, police and prosecutors. Rotterdam's cybercrime team took over the case and (according to the police release) had found the group's servers within days after the investigation.

The scam calls began with a recorded voice claiming that there had been suspicious activity on the account and asked the person to press 1 if they did not recognise it. Victims were then talked into installing an app and holding their bank card against their phone.

Police estimated about 170 victims and about €380,000 in losses involved in the incident over the three weeks period of investigation. The victims resided across Netherlands and were in diverse age range. Dozens were in their twenties and thirties.

## The AI claim

The claim comes from the police. The release states that the fraudsters used AI to make dozens of sound clips with different voices for different banks, companies and scenarios. One local report, EdeStad, covers the arrests but does not mention AI.

The release does not state any tool and confirm whether the voices imitated real bank employee or were generically synthetic. It also does not claim the app was built with AI.

## How the money was taken

The app is the part police describe as new. It recognized the chip of the victim's bank card through the phone and passed the data to the same app on another phone. The scammer holding that second phone stood at a cash machine or a payment terminal and used the victim's card data. Police claim they had never come across this method before.

This entry leaves out anything beyond what the police published.

## Arrests and detention

Both men were arrested on October 6, 2026. Police describe the 23-year-old, from Vaassen, as the suspected technical lead who provided instructions to accomplices. The 25-year-old, from Ede, is suspected of a coordinating role. Police took the servers offline and seized electronic devices (phones and laptops).

On October 20, 2026, the pre-trial chamber of the Rotterdam District Court ordered both men held for at least 90 more days. Police announced the arrests the next day, which is the date on this entry. Neither man has been named, and the release does not list the offences they are suspected of. Nothing has been tested in court.

## What police told the public

Banks never ask customers to install an app, hold a card against a phone or provide a PIN. When suspicious, police advise hanging up and calling the bank printed on the back of the card. Anyone who has fallen victim of the scam should contact the bank to block the card and file a report.

## Open

**Charges** No offence or statute has been published. The 90-day order runs to about January 18, 2027, and a first hearing should follow.

**Roles** EdeStad describes the two men's roles the other way round from the police release. This entry follows the police.

**The voices** Which tool made the recordings, and whether real people's voices were copied, has not been made public.

**Other suspects** The people who collected the cash at machines have not been reported arrested.

**The data** Police are still going through the seized servers, so the victim count may rise.
