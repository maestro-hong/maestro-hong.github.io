## What happened

According to a news release from the Seminole County Sheriff's Office, a deputy was inside a retail store in Lake Mary on March 24, 2026 when a man approached him and said he had seen several people enter the deputy's marked patrol vehicle in the parking lot. The man showed him a video on his phone, approximately three seconds long, which appeared to show two people entering the vehicle. The deputy checked the vehicle and found nothing disturbed or stolen. After reviewing store surveillance footage, investigators determined that no one approached the vehicle during the time the man described. Detectives concluded that the video had been fabricated and that the reported incident did not occur.

Detectives identified Alexis Martínez-Arizala from Lake Worth, Florida, as the man who showed the deputy the video, the release says. It adds that investigators learned he posted content about the encounter on his social media accounts "in an apparent attempt to gain attention and create viral content".

The release says a warrant was signed on March 27 and that on April 8 an officer assigned to a U.S. Marshals Service task force arrested Martínez-Arizala in San Juan, Puerto Rico. The release lists four charges, two felonies, tampering with or fabricating physical evidence and unlawful use of a two-way communication device, and two misdemeanors, false report of a crime and giving false information to a law enforcement officer. He will be held on a $7,000 bond once he is extradited to Seminole County, the release says.

## The AI claim

The first paragraph of the release says the video was "believed to have been created using artificial intelligence". The release names no tool, and its headline drops the hedge and calls the video an "AI-Generated Deepfake Video". Elsewhere the release says detectives concluded the video had been fabricated.

WESH says investigators determined the video was an AI-generated deepfake, and WFLX's headline calls it an AI video.

## Open

**The belief** The release does not say who believed the video was made with AI, or on what evidence. In the text we read, WESH and WFLX give no basis either.

**The video** WESH reports that the office has not released it because of safety concerns, so we could not look at it or check how it was made.

**The check** The release says investigators reviewed store surveillance footage. It does not say what first made the deputy or the detectives doubt the video.

**The court record** We have not read the arrest affidavit, the charging document or the docket. The charges are allegations, and the three sources we read report no plea, trial or conviction and give no account from him.

**The extradition** WFLX reported on April 14 that extradition had not happened as of Monday, April 13. One search for later coverage found nothing after April.

**The date** Our incident date is April 8, 2026, the day of the arrest and the release. The video was shown to the deputy on March 24.
