Methodology

How entries are created

What gets logged

A case is logged when a public source alleges that artificial intelligence played a material role in committing, attempting or enabling a crime. The allegation does not have to be proven. How well it is supported is recorded separately (see How each case is coded), and some entries exist to test a claim that turns out to be thin. Entries are built only from public sources and each source is tiered by how close it is to the events.

Source tiers

Every source in each entry carries a tier. Each tier describes where the information originated:

P   Primary
Court filings, indictments, judgments, agency releases, regulator notices, official statistics.
S   Secondary
News organisations reporting on a primary record or their own reporting.
T   Tertiary
Vendor blogs, trade press, aggregators. Often the only available source.

Enforcement status

Each status records the furthest enforcement step that is publicly recorded. Open cases are re-checked on a schedule, and each entry shows when it was last updated.

  • Reported only — no enforcement action publicly recorded.
  • Investigation opened — an agency has confirmed it is investigating.
  • Charges laid — charges or an indictment filed.
  • Sanctions or seizure — designations, asset seizure, infrastructure takedown.
  • Platform action — account, model or service-level enforcement by a provider.
  • Civil litigation — private or state civil proceedings underway.
  • Convicted — conviction entered, whether or not sentencing is complete.
  • No human offender — no person directed the conduct, so no prosecution has been brought. Whether a developer or operator could be held liable has not been tested.

How each case is coded

Besides category and status, each entry records three judgments, so cases can be compared on the same terms. They are this record's assessment of the public sources, and they change when the sources do.

Notes on each entry

Every entry carries one short note. Mechanism explains what made the conduct possible. Claim check tests a specific claim against the sources. Numbers check explains what a reported figure does and does not show. Open question names the main unresolved issue.

Naming

An accused or suspected person is named only after a court document or an official agency release has named them. This record does not attribute crimes to anyone. Where an entry disputes how a case has been characterised, the accused is not named even if police have released the name. Officials, researchers and experts quoted in public sources are named as those sources name them.

What is excluded

  • Information from non-public sources.
  • Operational detail that could help someone commit an offence.
  • In sexual exploitation cases, any description of the material or how it was made. These entries cover detection, charges and outcomes only.

Dates

The date shown on each entry is the date the case first became public through an enforcement step or disclosure: an arrest, a charge or a company's announcement. Where sources give only a month, the first of the month is used and the entry says so.

Use of AI tools

Unio Analytics uses AI tools to help find and translate sources and to check drafts. Every fact is checked against the cited source, and every judgment is the author's.
