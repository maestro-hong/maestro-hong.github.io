Statutes

Which of these laws has ever been used?

Legislatures have passed many laws aimed at AI-enabled conduct. This page asks one question of each: has it produced a criminal conviction, and if not, what law did?

So far, laws written for AI have produced one located conviction, under the US TAKE IT DOWN Act. The other convictions in this record came under general criminal law, some of it older than the internet. South Korea's deepfake provisions are the exception: they are being used at volume.

A count of zero does not mean a law is useless. Most of these laws are recent, and prosecutors tend to use the charges they know. The count describes what the public record shows today.

What the counts mean

A number is the count of criminal convictions this record has located under that statute for AI-enabled conduct. In use marks a general statute that has carried such a conviction without being written for it. n/a marks a regulatory instrument with no criminal provisions to count. High volume marks a statute being applied at a scale where individual cases are not separately tracked here.

Absence of a located conviction is not proof that none exists. Court records are uneven across jurisdictions and most are not searchable in English. Where a conviction is found, this page will change and the entry it came from will be linked. Corrections to {{ site.author.email }}.

Charged marks a statute with a case pending and no conviction yet.
